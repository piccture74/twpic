# Fascia Manipulation Assessment Chart and Clinical Reasoning
## The Assessment Chart as the Core of Fascia Manipulation
In Fascia Manipulation（FM），I regard the assessment chart as the core of the course。It is the method I use to assess，evaluate，and decide what needs to be treated。
Without the assessment chart，I would say that I am not truly doing FM。The FM chart is the method and framework I use to analyze the patient。It helps me organize the entire evaluation so that I can define what needs treatment。
Many other physicians and clinicians also use this chart。In the end，they may use shock wave therapy，a needle on the point，or another technique。However，what matters more is not only how the point is treated，but how I decide which point should be treated。
## Structure of the Chart
When I look at the chart，I can clearly see several parts。
The upper part is the medical record，which is basically the patient’s history。
Below that is the movement verification。This includes the tests，including orthopedic tests，that I teach in order to try to exacerbate the symptom。
Then there is the palpatory verification。This refers to palpation and how I understand what is wrong。
At the end is the treatment section，where I write down what needs to be treated。
## Personal Data and Its Importance
I start from the beginning，with the patient’s data and personal information。This includes：
- Name。
- Surname。
- Date of birth。
- Occupation。
- Sport。
This information is very important because I need to understand why the patient has symptoms。For example，I need to understand how much sport the patient is doing：twice a week，once a month，running 10K，or running 20K。This helps me understand whether there is overuse。
A desk worker，for example，may stay in the same position all day without supporting the elbow。This is a typical and clear example of overuse that can generate symptoms。
## Identifying the Side of Pain
After that，I ask the patient to point to where the pain is。I try to ask the patient to describe the symptom，which we call the side of pain。This means the area where there is the greatest pain，or the main reason that brings the patient to see me。
I describe this using the typical nomenclature described in the biomechanical model。For example，if the patient points to the humerus，I describe it as：
- Segment：humerus。
- Location：anterior。
- Side：right。
## Duration，Trauma，Pain Scale，and Recurrence
For duration，there are two columns。The first column describes how long the current acute condition has been present。The second column describes when the patient had the symptom for the first time。
The letter “T” means trauma，or I may write a question mark。I need to determine whether there was a trauma when the symptom started。
VRS means verbal rating scale。I ask how the pain is right now。It may be zero at the moment。I also ask what the maximum pain is；for example，it may be eight。
I also need to understand whether the pain is recurrent or constant。For example，if the pain goes from zero to eight，I need to know how often it reaches eight。It may happen twice a week，once a month，or it may be constant。
It is very important to describe the patient’s symptom as clearly as possible。I need to guide the patient and ask properly so that I can fill in the chart accurately。
## Examples of Symptom Description
One patient may be recorded as：colles，retro，bilateral，six months，question mark，two to six，one per month。
This means neck pain on the back side，symmetrical and equal on both sides，lasting for six months，without any clear trauma。The pain is currently two，it can go up to six，and once a month it increases to six。
Another patient may have a shoulder problem on the lateral side，on the right。It started two years ago with a trauma。The pain is now four and can go up to eight。In particular，it gets worse twice a week。
Another example is low back pain。The low back pain is more around the five o’clock to seven o’clock area，but more on the left。It has lasted for three months without trauma。The pain is eight，constant，and always present。
This is a simple and straightforward way to describe symptoms。Many people now use this method to describe pain because it is simple and fast。
## Defining Real Trauma
When I talk about trauma，I need to make sure it is real trauma。For example，if the patient says，“I went to the gym，I lifted a heavy weight，and since then my shoulder hurts，”I need to ask whether that is truly trauma。It is not。
When I use the word trauma，it must be a major trauma that surely altered the tissue。Examples include a fall，a fracture，or another major event。If I am not sure，I should put a question mark。
This is important because it is much easier to define the history and the sequelae of a real trauma，since that is the beginning of the problem。
However，most of the time，the event only triggered the symptom，while poor biomechanics were already present in that area。That situation is definitely more difficult to understand。If I misunderstand one situation as the other，the evaluation may become unclear。
## Assessment Process and Clinical Reasoning
If I do not complete the assessment process correctly，the entire evaluation can become incorrect。Therefore，I only write down “trauma” when I am sure that a major event started the whole problem。If I am not sure，I mark it with a question mark。
In the chart，there is more detailed information explaining exactly what I need to write in each box。If I am not clear about what should be written，I should review that section carefully。
## Painful Movement
After that，I need to describe what is called the painful movement，meaning what makes the pain worse。
For example，if the patient’s pain ranges from two to six，I need to ask what makes it worse。It may be a specific movement or activity。The patient may say，“The worst thing is carrying my son’s bag。”In that case，I write down，“Carrying my son’s bag，”because that is the question I will ask again next time。
I should not write only technical terms such as elevation or rotation。I should write in plain English，in a way that people can understand，and in a way that I will understand what to ask at the next visit。
This is very important because，from the patient’s point of view，the pain may seem the same。For example，the patient may say，“The pain is the same。”I may then ask，“How is it when you carry your son’s bag？”The patient may answer，“That does not hurt anymore，but now bringing the scapula hurts。”For me，that is a completely different motion and a completely different structure。For the patient，however，it may feel like the same problem。
## Concomitant or Concurrent Pain
Next，I need to understand what is called concomitant or concurrent pain。A patient may come to the office with only a shoulder problem，but may also have other symptoms。These symptoms may not be as important，may not be as severe，or may not bother the patient as much，but the patient would still be happy if they were treated。These symptoms can also be important for me。
For example，the patient may have elbow pain，neck pain，or upper back pain。Some patients may have many painful areas。To narrow the symptoms down to two，the chart provides only two lines，because I need to select only two symptoms。
The most important way to narrow down the symptoms is to choose the symptoms that started before the first onset of the main painful area。
For example，the patient may say，“I am here because of a shoulder problem。It has been present for two weeks，but it actually started five years ago。”I then need to ask，“Did you have any other symptom that started before five years ago and is still active？”
The patient may say，“Yes，the elbow。It has been present for about seven years，since I started playing tennis。I also have neck pain，but that is more recent，about a couple of years。”If I do not have enough space，I do not choose the neck pain。I choose the elbow pain，because I want to select the symptom that may be the origin of the painful area。
If there are still too many symptoms，I choose the ones that are anatomically related。For example，I do not focus on pain in the fifth finger of the other hand，but I may choose a wrist problem or an upper back problem。
If there are still too many symptoms，I choose the one that is more intense or more noticeable。In this way，I can narrow the symptoms down and select only the ones that help me understand why the patient woke up five years ago with a shoulder problem。
## Extremities
This is still not enough。I also need to evaluate what are called the extremities。The extremities are very important。These include the caput，digits，and feet。
At the fascial level，the deep fascia fuses with the periosteum at the end，and it fuses with the galea capitis at the top of the head。This means that when there is too much tension，the tension pulls，and eventually it starts to pull on the periosteum。
This can generate minor or major deformation。Examples include hammer toes，valgus，trigger finger，rigidity of the foot，and large calluses。It can also include numbness，pins and needles，headaches，migraines，temporomandibular joint disorders，vertigo，and other sensations in the extremities。
These findings can be very important for many reasons。First，they can help me decide which plane should be treated。Second，they can tell me how severe the patient is。The more extremities are altered，the more tension is present throughout the body，and the more severe，chronic，or critical the patient may be。
A patient who has all five extremities altered may be a patient who，in a couple of months，could become stuck in bed，or who has had many acute events in the past。When I treat this kind of patient，there may be a lot of radiation throughout the body。
By contrast，the same symptom in a patient who has no symptoms in the extremities is more manageable。There is less radiation，the treatment is easier to manage，and there is no exacerbation of other symptoms during treatment。Therefore，symptoms in the extremities can be very important。
## Examples of Extremity Symptoms
One example is caput one lateral right，meaning a small pain in that area。
Another example is caput three to caput two，extra left more。This means the symptom goes from caput three up to caput two，like a half circle above the ear，more on the left。
There may also be digit or foot symptoms in a specific area。
Caput symptoms can also be in the opposite direction，such as caput two to caput three。The pain may start in one area and spread backward。I need to be specific because these symptoms can be critical and must be followed over time。
The image or body map is very helpful because I can map where the symptom is。For example，I can mark where the headache or migraine is。I can also identify which finger has numbness or which toes are involved。This is very important，and I need to keep the image in mind because I will use it to describe the symptom。
## Previous Pain
I also need to understand whether there was any previous pain。This refers to pain that was intense or severe in the past but is now gone。
There may be many previous pains，even too many。I need to decide which ones are important。The easiest way is to select the symptoms that started before the first event of the main painful area。
For example，the patient’s shoulder problem may have been present for two weeks，but it started five years ago。I need to ask，“Did you have any major problem that started before five years ago？”The previous problem may have ended one month ago or two years ago；that does not matter。
In this way，I try to narrow down the history。If there are still too many previous pains，they must have some anatomical relationship。
The space for pain recurrence can also become a space for notes。In that section，I write down when the symptom started and when it ended。
## Surgery，Fracture，Internal Dysfunction，and Other Important History
Surgery，fracture，and internal dysfunction are still very important。
When discussing surgery，I need to remember that surgery over the coordinating center creates many more problems than surgery outside the coordinating center。Emergency surgery is also more important to know about than scheduled surgery。For example，a C-section or appendectomy done as an emergency is more likely to create adhesions and later problems。
For fractures，all fractures should be considered，especially if they were important。More generally，I consider any trauma that took a long time to recover。
For example，if I had an ankle sprain and after two weeks I was running as before，I do not pay much attention to it。But if an ankle sprain took three months before I could run again，that matters。I need to imagine how many compensations the body could build in three months to allow the person to walk in a certain way。These are the traumas I must take into consideration：traumas that take longer to recover。
Medication is also important，especially anticoagulants and antiaggregants，because they may matter in relation to trauma。
Even at this level，I should start thinking about internal dysfunctions such as bloating，reflux，gastritis，colitis，and constipation。These symptoms are strongly correlated with the visceral fascia，which is continuous with the musculoskeletal fascia。This may seem early now，but patients will begin to tell me that they have these symptoms。This is typical of a level three patient with a visceral-somatic problem or a somato-visceral problem，which will be discussed in the future。These conditions are much more common than they may seem。
## Surgery and Deep Fascia
I also need to consider how surgery relates to the deep fascia。If a procedure does not reach the deep fascia，it may not be important。For example，if something superficial is removed without reaching the deep fascia，I do not need to focus on it。But if it was a melanoma and the surgery reached the deep fascia，then it becomes very important。
The location of the incision or hole is important，even in laparoscopy。If the hole is over an intra-abdominal or relevant biomechanical area，it can strongly affect biomechanics。A hole in the linea alba is less critical because the linea alba is a natural fusion area，so it is not as significant mechanically。
Plastic surgery can be problematic if it affects the deep fascia。For example，if the procedure is below the pectoralis major，it can be a problem。Old orthopedic surgeries also affect the deep fascia。
## Collecting Data in Order
When collecting data，I need to go in order。I should write things down and assess by segment so that I can make a clear analysis of the body。
I should not repeat trauma。If I write a trauma symbol at the top，I do not need to repeat the same trauma again in the trauma box。
I should not pay attention to very small events。In this way，I can create a clear chart。If the chart is clear，it will guide me toward treatment。
## Timeline
Once I have written everything down，the chart prepares the timeline。The timeline shows what happened first，second，third，fourth，and fifth。It shows the organization of the events。
This is very important because it helps me understand the compensations the body has generated over time。It is critical for developing what is called the hypothesis。
## Hypothesis
The hypothesis is the most important and most difficult moment of the entire evaluation process。It requires me to stop，think，and choose which segment I want to evaluate through movement，and which segment I want to evaluate through palpation。
Choosing the movement is easier。Choosing palpation is more difficult because palpation is directly related to the hypothesis。
For example，the patient may have a shoulder problem that seems to have been present for two weeks，but the patient actually woke up with the shoulder problem five years ago。I need to ask：Why did this happen？Where is it coming from？
It may be coming from epicondylitis that has been present for seven years，from neck pain that has been present for two years，or from a rib fracture that happened three years ago。
In this case，the hypothesis may be clear：it comes from the epicondylitis that has been present for seven years，because the problem must be older in order to generate a more proximal compensation。In this case，the shoulder problem is an ascending problem。
If there had been a whiplash or car accident eight or six years earlier，I would need to choose between the car accident and the epicondylitis by understanding which one was worse。If there was only a car accident，it would be a descending problem。
This part is very important。It basically defines whether the problem is ascending or descending。It gives me the key to understand the whole history of the patient and where to look for the major densification that must be treated in order to restore biomechanics。
For example，a knee problem can come from an ankle sprain or from a low back problem。If the knee is the painful area，I need to decide whether to palpate the coxa segment or the talus segment。The genu segment will be evaluated for sure。
After selecting two segments，I palpate the coordinating centers：extra，retro，lateral，intra，ante，and medio。I try to understand which densification is worst in each area。Then I understand which planes are more affected，and which plane created the worst and most severe disorder for the patient today。That is the plane I will treat，because later we treat one plane at a time。
## Knee Pain Example
Consider a patient who has had right knee pain for five years。The patient has also had low back pain for three years and an ankle fracture on the same side six years ago。
Which segment do I want to palpate？
I palpate the genu for sure。Next，I palpate the talus，because the talus injury is six years old and definitely older than the low back pain。I palpate these two segments using transversal palpation：ante，intra，latero，extra，and retro in both。
If I find a lot of densification in the talus，I confirm my hypothesis，and I know that the result will be good。
If，for some reason，the talus is empty and there is no densification，I need to ask the patient，“How was this fracture？”The patient may say，“I had a cast，but after two weeks I took it off because it was really just a greenstick fracture，and I was running easily。It was nothing serious。”
Then I need to ask，“How was the low back pain？”The patient may say，“It has only been three years，but it bothered me a lot。Actually，ten years ago I was stuck in bed and had many injections。”This means that the low back pain is much older。Then I palpate the pelvis，find a lot of densification，and understand that the knee problem is not ascending but descending from there。
Palpation must confirm the hypothesis。This is very important because trauma creates densification，and the body tries to compensate。In this way，the body creates a new balance。After some time，a symptom may go away，but the new pathological balance may create dysfunction in another body segment，then another symptom，and then another symptom。
If the patient comes to the office with a symptom and I do not treat the original area，the situation can recur。Therefore，I need to understand the origin of the problem in order to restore full biomechanics and make sure recurrence does not happen。
## Complex Example：Mario
Sometimes the situation can be difficult。For example，Mario is a desk worker，but he was a professional basketball player。He came in with upper back pain on the right side for two months。It had started six months earlier without trauma。The intensity reached seven to eight in the morning。Getting out of bed made it worse。
He also had low back pain，more on the right side，since 2002，without trauma。The intensity was six to seven，and standing made it worse。
He had plastic surgery，including shortening of the mandible。He had a fracture of the fifth finger on the right in the 1980s。He had a cut of the fibular nerve on the right in 1978。He had a double discectomy at L3-L4 and L5-S1 in 2003。He had a meniscectomy on the left in 1992。He had a right Achilles sprain in 1979。He also had an Achilles tendon rupture on the left。
This is very complicated。If the chart is not clear，I can turn the chart over，draw a line，and write down all the symptoms in order to create a timeline。
When I do this，I see the following sequence：
- Trauma in 1978 in the right knee。
- Trauma in 1979 in the right ankle。
- Finger fracture，which is not very important。
- Plastic surgery。
- Some symptoms in the right and left knee。
- The left knee symptoms may have appeared because the other leg already had two traumas，so he started carrying weight on the other side，creating overuse symptoms。
- Low back pain without trauma，because he started shifting weight，causing overuse。
- Low back surgery。
- Operation on the ankle。
- Mechanics became poor，and he started carrying and compensating，creating problems in another area。
- Upper back symptoms appeared。
To understand this clearly，I need to go back to the beginning of the problem，such as the genu area，and also evaluate where the symptom is now，such as the thorax or pelvis。
I may evaluate combinations such as：
- Genu and thorax。
- Genu and pelvis。
- Talus and thorax。
- Talus and pelvis。
Any of these combinations may be appropriate because I go to the oldest important issue and to the current painful area。
## Deciding What to Evaluate
When I decide what to evaluate，I need to decide what to evaluate for movement and what to evaluate for palpation。
For movement，the decision is simple。I choose the painful area and the concomitant pain，because the modification aims to reproduce the patient’s symptom。It is easier to reproduce the patient’s symptom if I choose the painful area，perform movement there，and eventually include the concomitant pain that is related and that I know I will treat together。
For palpation，the situation is more complex because I need to select two segments。One is the painful area，and the other is the oldest important area indicated by the hypothesis。
## Movement Evaluation
Movement has the purpose of reproducing the symptom。I can test active movement，passive movement，and movement against resistance。
I can use asterisks to grade the findings：
- One asterisk：slight pain or deficit。
- Two asterisks：moderate pain or major deficit。
- Three asterisks：the patient is not able to perform the movement。
This is important because at the end of the session，I can repeat the test if I want to objectify the result。
## Palpation Evaluation
For palpation，I need to go to the oldest important area。I palpate the painful area for sure，and I also choose another area according to my hypothesis。If it is a descending problem，I go up。If it is an ascending problem，I go down and choose another segment。
If the patient has spreading pain，I can choose the beginning and the end of the symptom。Even if the radiation goes to the toes or fingers，I should choose the carpus or talus because they are useful，provide more information，and are easier to palpate。
I should avoid choosing neighboring segments because two neighboring segments influence each other and do not provide good data。I should choose segments that are not neighbors。In this way，I can also get an idea of what is happening in the segment between them and already have an idea of a third segment。
The hypothesis is not 100 percent certain。Palpation confirms it。I need a hypothesis to select the segment。If palpation of that segment reveals densification，then I confirm the hypothesis。
## Professional Soccer Player Example
Another example is a 24-year-old professional soccer player who comes in with low back pain，more on the right side。
I first considered a patient who had an extra-pelvic-type problem，described as being around five to seven in intensity。The issue had started three months earlier，although there had also been a problem one year earlier。There was no trauma。At the time of assessment，the pain was zero，but it could increase up to eight when it “jumped”。
There was also a right hip problem that had started three years earlier，without trauma。The pain could go up to five，although it was zero at the time of assessment。Stop-and-go movements made the symptoms worse。
The patient had previously had surgery for myopia，but this was not important and could even be ignored。The patient also had an ankle fracture twenty years earlier，when the pain was four on the right。
Because the patient was pain-free during the assessment，I performed movement testing to provoke the symptoms。The goal was to exacerbate the typical symptom so that，after treatment，I could immediately verify whether there was a result。
I performed pelvic mobilization，and I was fortunate that，with the extra-pelvic movement on the right，I reproduced the patient’s symptom。I was not completely satisfied with that，so I also tested the movement in the coxa region，because the coxa was on the same side。In this way，I knew that，on that day，I would likely improve both symptoms because they were related。The intra-coxa movement on the right also exacerbated the symptom。
At that point，I had two movement verifications that reproduced the patient’s symptoms。This gave me a way to prove the result immediately in the office。
## The Importance of Verification
I must remember that，as soon as I reproduce the patient’s symptom，I can stop。However，it is important that I am able to exacerbate the patient’s typical symptom in the office，because this is the only way to prove the result。
After movement verification，I begin palpation。As already stated，palpation is the most important part。
The basic rating system is as follows：
- One asterisk means I find a densification，usually sharp and painful。
- Two asterisks indicate a jump sign，with major densification。The patient jumps because it is very acute。
- Three asterisks indicate referred pain，meaning the pain spreads。
This is a very elementary and basic way to define the rating。Once I have a little more experience，especially in 1B，the rating will be based more on how rigid the tissue is。
Sometimes，as will be seen in strategy，the point is not painful at all。This is called a latent point。I need to warm it up a little，and then it becomes painful。Therefore，what I feel is more important than what the patient feels。
Referred pain is the best finding I can have，especially if the pain is perceived on the painful side。
The only sensation I must avoid is an electric shock。If the patient feels an electric shock with the same frequency as my knuckle，it means I am outside the coordination center and I am going transversely across a nerve。
## Palpation and Hypothesis in the First Case
After completing the movement assessment，I proceeded with palpation。In this case，there had been a fracture on the same side。I hypothesized that the problem was ascending from the ankle sprain or ankle fracture。
However，when I performed transverse palpation in the pelvis and talus，there was almost nothing in the talus：only one asterisk。This suggested that the old fracture was like a greenstick fracture in a very young child who was not even walking yet。A fracture at that age did not generate any problem。By contrast，a fracture at twelve years old would generate a problem。
At the biopsy，there was a lot of ossification in the pelvis。This indicated overuse in that region。
To be sure，I palpated a third segment。In this case，it was the coxa。The coxa again pointed toward the sagittal plane。
When I counted the asterisks only from palpation，I checked where there was more densification：sagittal，frontal，or horizontal plane。The sagittal plane had more asterisks，so the sagittal plane had to be treated。
On that day，I would treat only the sagittal plane，meaning only the anterior-retro points，and nothing in the other planes，even if the movement restriction looked worse in the horizontal plane。
I must not trust movement。Movement will never tell me the truth。Movement may show that one direction is worse，but palpation may indicate the sagittal plane。I must treat the sagittal plane，and the movement restriction will improve because the movement verification is sensitive but not specific。
It is sensitive because it tells me that something is wrong，but it is not specific because it does not tell me what is wrong。When I move the patient，I stretch and pull everything。Therefore，I should not be surprised if treating the correct plane improves not only that specific movement verification but also the other one。
## Counting Asterisks and Choosing One Plane
When counting，I must remember that one box with three stars has more value than three boxes with one star。To make evaluation faster，I basically count only the boxes with three stars。
Once I choose a plane，I stay in that plane。I do not change planes during the same treatment。The reason is methodological。If the patient returns next time and says，“I am better，”I continue in the same plane。If the patient says，“It was better for a few days，but now it is like before，”then I change the plane。
With Fascia Manipulation，in two treatments，I can usually obtain a very good result if I follow the method。If I start mixing planes，the treatment becomes unbalanced。I may unbalance two planes，and the patient may start having symptoms。Even in the best scenario，if the patient gets better，I will not know how to proceed next time。

# Case Studies
## Case 1：Twenty-Six-Year-Old Gymnast
I considered a twenty-six-year-old gymnast who came in because of right-sided low back pain that had been present for seven years。There was no trauma。The pain ranged from two to eight and worsened with extension。
There was also low back pain，more on the right，for five years。There was no trauma。The pain ranged from two to six and worsened with extension。
I used two separate lines because the origin and intensity were different。If everything had been the same，I would have written lumbi／pelvi。That would mean the pain spread from lumbi to pelvi，or from pelvi to lumbi。Since the details were different in this case，I had to use two different lines。
The patient also had bilateral coccyalgia for ten years。The pain ranged from zero to ten，without trauma，and was worsened by flexion and intra-abduction。
There was a left tibial fracture ten years earlier and a clavicle fracture twelve years earlier。
The timing was：
- Coccyalgia：ten years。
- Low back pain：five and seven years。
- Tibial fracture：ten years。
- Clavicle fracture：twelve years。
It is true that the clavicle was the oldest event，but the coxa-related problem was slightly older than the other symptoms。Below that，even on the opposite side，there was the talus。If someone does not walk properly，the weight must be carried on the opposite leg，causing overuse。
In my opinion，the clavicle was not related to this problem，but the tibial or ankle-related issue was related。I could ask how long recovery took，whether crutches were needed，and whether the patient had to walk improperly for weeks。If the answer was yes，then the patient probably over-carried weight on the other side。This could distribute stress through the crutches，pelvis，and lumbar region。
My hypothesis was an ascending problem that crossed the midline。Therefore，I knew I had to work on the system as a whole because the findings were related。
I performed movement verification in the lumbar region and in the coxa。I did not have to test the pelvis separately because the brackets showed that an antero-lumbar movement referred pain into another segment。Brackets mean that when I perform a movement，the pain does not occur in that same segment but in another one。In this case，by testing lumbar and coxa，the pain probably spread into the pelvis。This saved time because I did not have to perform pelvic movement verification。
There were many movement restrictions，telling me that something was wrong。
I then moved to transverse palpation。I could have chosen the talus，but if the problem comes from the talus and goes upward，I must go to the coxa。Therefore，I decided to choose coxa and lumbi because they are not neighboring segments。
In any case，the pain would have to go through the coxa to cross the midline。With coxa palpation，I would be sure to find the dysfunction，because I would perceive the dysfunction coming up from the talus region。Choosing the talus would not have been a mistake，and I kept it as a possibility in case I did not have enough information to decide。
With the coccyx，however，I had a clear map because the sagittal plane looked the worst。
After that，I performed some longitudinal palpation up and down。There was a good number of asterisks。I could start treating ante-pelvis and ante-coccyx。Then，when the pain was already reduced and movement was better，I could treat the back，where the patient actually had pain。
The anterior area did not have pain，so it was not a problem。The patient might be surprised that I treated that area，but I would not be worried about using the elbow there because it was not symptomatic。This is a safe way to treat because I do not immediately touch the symptomatic area。I go there only when the patient already has about 50％ of the result。
## Case 2：Left Sciatic Pain for Thirty Years
I then considered a patient with left sciatic pain for thirty years。There was no trauma。The pain ranged from six to nine，was constant，and worsened with sitting。
There was also upper back and low back discomfort on the left side for ten years，without trauma。The pain ranged from zero to six and also worsened with sitting。
The patient had a headache from three to one，extra，shaped like a half moon，for five years。There was no trauma。The intensity ranged from zero to six。
There was also a bilateral foot problem，described as cramps，more on the right，for eight years。There was no trauma。The cramps were intense during the night。
The patient had an appendectomy thirty years earlier and a C-section eleven months earlier。There had also been knee trauma fifteen years earlier and a fracture of the fifth metatarsal on the right thirty-eight years earlier。
